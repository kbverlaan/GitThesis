# 2. Motivation & Context

Complex political order, entailing stable alliances, hierarchies and balance-of-power, are typically explained as products of high-level cognitive capabilities: trust, loyalty, shared norms or deliberate institutional design.

However, computational research in optimization, agent-based modelling and complex systems has repeatedly shown that highly structured collective behaviour can emerge from minimal agents purely optimizing reward-functions under material constraints.

These claims create a tension. Are semantic concepts like "loyalty" and "trust" causal generators of political order, or merely interprative labels applied to equilibria arised from incentive structures alone?

This thesis investigates whether elements of complex political order can be generated by agents that lack semantic understanding, and compare these dynamics to agents equipped with semantic priors via language models.

## Significance
If pure optimizers produce the same dynamics as semantically-aware agents, it could suggest political order emerges from physics of power.
If semantically-aware agents coordinate where optimizers fail, it could suggest culture and shared matter causally to political order.
