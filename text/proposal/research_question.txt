RESEARCH QUESTION FRAMING
=========================
Last updated: 2026-01-28 (v2 - Major revision based on confounding analysis)

================================================================================
KEY INSIGHT: THE CONFOUNDING PROBLEM
================================================================================
Previous framing risked confounding TWO variables:
  1. Decision-making architecture (optimization vs semantic reasoning)
  2. Goal structure (relative gains vs identity/norms)

If divergence occurred, we couldn't tell which variable caused it.

SOLUTION: Two-comparison experimental design
  - Comparison 1: Architecture effect (RL vs LLM-Control, SAME goals)
  - Comparison 2: Framing effect (LLM-Control vs LLM-Constructivist, SAME architecture)

================================================================================
MAIN RESEARCH QUESTION (REVISED)
================================================================================
"Can semantic-reasoning agents escape security dilemmas that trap
optimization-based agents?"

Or more formally:
"When optimization-based and semantic-reasoning agents engage the same
multi-round coordination game with identical incentive structures, do they
produce different equilibrium behaviors? Under what material constraints
do they converge vs. diverge?"

ALTERNATIVE PHRASINGS:
- "Does decision-making architecture (optimization vs semantic reasoning)
   affect the ability to escape security dilemmas in multi-agent systems?"

- "Under what conditions does semantic reasoning enable cooperation that
   pure optimization cannot achieve (or vice versa)?"


================================================================================
EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN: TWO COMPARISONS
================================================================================

COMPARISON 1: ARCHITECTURE EFFECT
---------------------------------
Agents: RL vs LLM-Control
Goal: BOTH optimize relative gains / security
Difference: How they reason (optimization vs semantic processing)

Question: Does reasoning architecture alone produce divergence?

Possible findings:
  - If RL ≈ LLM-Control: Architecture doesn't matter when goals are same
  - If RL ≠ LLM-Control: Architecture matters even with identical goals


COMPARISON 2: FRAMING EFFECT
-----------------------------
Agents: LLM-Control vs LLM-Constructivist
Architecture: BOTH use semantic reasoning (same LLM)
Difference: How goals are framed (relative gains vs identity/norms)

Question: Does constructivist framing change outcomes?

Possible findings:
  - If LLM-Control ≈ LLM-Constructivist: Framing doesn't matter
  - If LLM-Control ≠ LLM-Constructivist: Constructivist framing adds something


COMBINED INTERPRETATION:
------------------------
| RL vs LLM-Control | LLM-Control vs Constructivist | Interpretation |
|-------------------|-------------------------------|----------------|
| Converge          | Converge                      | Material structure dominates all |
| Converge          | Diverge                       | Framing matters, not architecture |
| Diverge           | Converge                      | Architecture matters, not framing |
| Diverge           | Diverge                       | Both architecture AND framing matter |


================================================================================
THEORETICAL FRAMING (COMPRESSED)
================================================================================
Keep IR theory but drastically compress it. Focus on ONE theoretical problem:

THE SECURITY DILEMMA:
In anarchic systems (no central authority), agents cannot perfectly
distinguish defensive from offensive capability. This creates a tragic
dynamic: individually rational arming leads to mutual arms races that
leave all agents worse off.

TWO COMPETING HYPOTHESES:

1. OPTIMIZATION HYPOTHESIS (Materialist Logic):
   Security dilemmas are difficult to escape through optimization.
   Because agents cannot distinguish threats from defenses, arms races
   emerge as equilibrium behavior. Cooperation remains fragile.

2. SEMANTIC REASONING HYPOTHESIS (Ideational Logic):
   Security dilemmas can be transcended through semantic reasoning.
   Through repeated signaling and semantic interpretation of history,
   agents can develop mutual trust enabling cooperation despite
   material incentives to defect.

WHY THIS MATTERS:
- Not just about IR theory - applies to any multi-agent coordination
- Security dilemmas appear in: international relations, market competition,
  organizational dynamics, AI safety (multi-agent systems)
- Testing whether reasoning architecture affects coordination outcomes


================================================================================
GAME DESIGN: WHY IT'S ALREADY THEORETICALLY RICH
================================================================================
Your game naturally embodies the security dilemma without needing to
force IR theory onto it:

| Action        | What it does                    | Theoretical significance        |
|---------------|--------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Invest-Self   | Create value for yourself      | Economic growth, private gain  |
| Transfer      | Give resources to other        | Costly signaling of trust      |
| Arm-Self      | Build military capability      | Ambiguous: defensive or offensive? |
| Alliance      | Arm another agent              | Commitment signal, dependence  |
| Attack        | Seize resources through force  | Exploitation, conflict         |

The ambiguity of Arm-Self IS the security dilemma:
- I arm myself → you don't know if I'm defending or preparing to attack
- You arm yourself in response → I interpret as threat
- Mutual arming consumes resources → both lose vs cooperation
- Neither can safely disarm → trapped in suboptimal equilibrium

The question: Can semantic reasoning interpret Arm-Self as defensive
and break the cycle? Can it interpret Transfer as trustworthy signal
rather than exploitable weakness?

WHAT YOU'RE TESTING (CLARIFIED)
================================================================================
NOT: "Which IR theory is right?"
NOT: "Do neorealist and constructivist agents behave differently?"
     (This was circular - we defined them differently)
NOT: "Can LLMs simulate states realistically?"

YES: "Do optimization and semantic reasoning produce different equilibria
      when facing identical material incentive structures?"
YES: "Can semantic reasoning escape security dilemmas?"
YES: "Under what parameter conditions do they converge vs diverge?"
YES: "Which comparison (architecture vs framing) explains more variance?"


================================================================================
WHY THIS IS STRONGER
================================================================================
1. NOT CIRCULAR: Both convergence and divergence are interesting findings
   - Convergence: Material incentives dominate reasoning differences
   - Divergence: Reasoning architecture shapes outcomes

2. ISOLATES VARIABLES: Two comparisons let you identify what matters
   - Architecture effect (RL vs LLM-Control)
   - Framing effect (LLM-Control vs LLM-Constructivist)

3. FALSIFIABLE: Clear predictions for each comparison

4. BROADLY RELEVANT: Speaks to CS, game theory, organizational behavior,
   not just IR scholars

5. COMPRESSED THEORY: Security dilemma framing is sufficient
   - Don't need full Waltz/Wendt review
   - Game mechanics embody the theoretical problem


================================================================================
METRICS (FOCUSED ON SECURITY DILEMMA DYNAMICS)
================================================================================
Primary metrics (security dilemma escape):
- Arms race indicator: ratio of Arm actions to Invest actions over time
- Cooperation emergence: sustained mutual Transfer relationships
- Conflict rate: Attack frequency per round
- Total welfare: sum of all resources (higher = more cooperation)

Secondary metrics (mechanism identification):
- Trust development: P(Transfer | received Transfer last round)
- Spiral dynamics: P(Arm | other Armed) - arms race indicator
- Alliance stability: duration of mutual Alliance relationships
- Betrayal rate: P(Attack | previous Alliance partner)

Comparison metrics:
- Time to first stable cooperation (if achieved)
- Time to first arms race (if occurs)
- Equilibrium type: cooperative, arms race, mixed, cycling
