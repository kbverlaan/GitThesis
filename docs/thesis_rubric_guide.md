# Thesis Rubric Scoring Guide

**Purpose**: This document maps the CLS Master Thesis Assessment Rubric to concrete actions and questions you should address to maximize your score. Use this as a checklist throughout your thesis work.

---

## Overview of Weights

| Category | Weight | Your Focus Level |
|----------|--------|------------------|
| **Research Work** | 35% | Demonstrated through doing, supervisor perception |
| **Thesis (Report)** | 50% | The written document - your main deliverable |
| **Presentation** | 15% | Oral defence - prepare separately |

---

## 1. RESEARCH WORK (35%)

*These criteria are assessed through your work process, not just the final document.*

### 1.1 Theoretical Knowledge (20% of Research Work)

**Criterion**: Did the student master the theory related to the research topic?

**Questions to ask yourself**:
- Can I explain Neorealism and Constructivism without notes?
- Can I articulate the key differences and points of contention?
- Do I understand the philosophical underpinnings (materialism vs idealism)?
- Can I explain why the Cold War ending challenged Neorealism?
- Do I understand ABM methodology deeply, not just superficially?
- Can I explain LLM architectures and their relevance to agent behavior?

**Evidence in thesis**:
- Literature review demonstrates deep engagement, not just summaries
- Correct use of theoretical terminology
- Nuanced discussion of theoretical debates
- Appropriate caveats and limitations acknowledged

**Actions**:
- [ ] Read primary sources (Waltz 1979, Wendt 1992/1999) completely
- [ ] Read critiques and responses to understand the full debate
- [ ] Study ABM methodology papers
- [ ] Understand LLM capabilities and limitations technically

---

### 1.2 Programming/Math Skills (20% of Research Work)

**Criterion**: Did the student demonstrate and/or develop programming and mathematical skills?

**Questions to ask yourself**:
- Can I explain every line of my simulation code?
- Can I modify the code to test new hypotheses without help?
- Do I understand the statistical methods I'm using?
- Can I justify my mathematical/computational choices?

**Evidence in thesis**:
- Well-documented, clean code (in appendix or repository)
- Appropriate statistical analysis
- Mathematical formalization where relevant
- Computational efficiency considerations

**Actions**:
- [ ] Write code incrementally, understanding each part
- [ ] Document code thoroughly in your own words
- [ ] Learn the statistics you use - don't just apply them blindly
- [ ] Consider computational complexity and scalability

---

### 1.3 Independence (20% of Research Work)

**Criterion**: Did the student perform and/or initiate high-quality activities independent from the supervisor?

**Questions to ask yourself**:
- What have I figured out on my own?
- What directions have I pursued without being told to?
- Can I identify problems and propose solutions independently?

**Evidence**:
- Supervisor can attest to independent work
- Novel directions or extensions you initiated
- Problem-solving without hand-holding

**Actions**:
- [ ] Keep a log of independent decisions and explorations
- [ ] Bring solutions to supervisor meetings, not just problems
- [ ] Pursue interesting tangents (within reason) on your own

---

### 1.4 Creativity and Originality (20% of Research Work)

**Criterion**: Did the student convincingly express and defend his/her own ideas to improve the research?

**Questions to ask yourself**:
- What is MY contribution to this research?
- What ideas have I proposed and defended?
- How is my approach different from existing work?
- Can I defend my choices under questioning?

**Evidence in thesis**:
- Clear articulation of novel contribution
- Defense of methodological choices
- Creative solutions to challenges
- Original interpretations or extensions

**Actions**:
- [ ] Articulate clearly what is novel about your approach
- [ ] Document your reasoning for key decisions
- [ ] Practice defending your choices out loud

---

### 1.5 Work Attitude (15% of Research Work)

**Criterion**: Did the student demonstrate a professional and responsible attitude?

**Actions**:
- [ ] Meet deadlines
- [ ] Communicate proactively with supervisor
- [ ] Manage time effectively
- [ ] Handle setbacks professionally

---

### 1.6 Cooperation (5% of Research Work)

**Criterion**: Did the student work well with others?

**Actions**:
- [ ] Be receptive to supervisor feedback
- [ ] Engage constructively with criticism
- [ ] Share ideas and be open to input

## 2. THESIS REPORT (50%)

*This is your main deliverable. Structure and content matter enormously.*

### 2.1 Relevance/Motivation (5% of Report)

**Criterion**: Was the subject introduced in correct scientific context with proper referencing? Was societal relevance recognized if applicable?

**What to include**:
- Clear positioning within IR theory and Computational Social Science
- Why this question matters (theoretically and possibly practically)
- Proper citations for all claims about the field

**Questions for yourself**:
- Why should anyone care about this research?
- What is the scientific gap I'm addressing?
- Is there broader relevance (policy, understanding conflict, etc.)?

**Actions**:
- [ ] Write introduction that clearly motivates the work
- [ ] Connect to real-world implications if appropriate
- [ ] Cite prior work that establishes importance

---

### 2.2 Use of Literature (20% of Report)

**Criterion**: Was quality and quantity of literature sufficient? Cited adequately and correctly presented?

**What to include**:
- Comprehensive literature review covering:
  - IR Theory (Neorealism, Constructivism, the debate)
  - ABM methodology in social science
  - LLM-based agents and multi-agent systems
  - Relevant computational social science work
- Proper citation format throughout
- Complete and correctly formatted reference list

**Questions for yourself**:
- Have I read the key works in each area?
- Am I citing primary sources, not just secondary summaries?
- Is my reference list complete and properly formatted?
- Am I engaging critically with the literature, not just listing it?

**Actions**:
- [ ] Identify 5-10 core papers in each area
- [ ] Read and understand them deeply
- [ ] Use consistent citation style
- [ ] Cross-reference your claims with citations

---

### 2.3 Scientific Question (10% of Report)

**Criterion**: Was there a clear and well-articulated scientific question?

**What to include**:
- Explicit research question(s) stated clearly
- Questions should be:
  - Specific enough to be answerable
  - Broad enough to be interesting
  - Connected to theoretical framework

**Questions for yourself**:
- Can I state my research question in one sentence?
- Is it clear what would count as an answer?
- How does it connect to the Neorealism/Constructivism debate?

**Draft your research question(s) here**:
- Main RQ: _______________________________________________
- Sub-questions (if any): ________________________________

**Actions**:
- [ ] Articulate research question early and refine it
- [ ] Ensure question is empirically addressable
- [ ] Connect question to theoretical stakes

---

### 2.4 Methods (20% of Report)

**Criterion**: Were methods described such that study could be repeated? Was justification given for method choice?

**What to include**:
- Complete description of computational model
- Agent architecture details (reproducible)
- Experimental design and parameters
- Justification for all methodological choices
- Enough detail for replication

**Questions for yourself**:
- Could someone replicate my study from the methods section alone?
- Have I justified WHY I chose this approach?
- Are all parameters and configurations documented?
- Have I addressed potential methodological criticisms?

**Structure suggestion**:
1. Model Overview (high-level description)
2. Agent Architecture (LLM setup, prompts, memory)
3. Environment Design (interaction structure, anarchy operationalization)
4. Experimental Design (what scenarios, how many runs, parameters)
5. Analysis Methods (what metrics, statistical tests)

**Actions**:
- [ ] Write methods section as you implement
- [ ] Document every decision and its rationale
- [ ] Include enough detail for replication
- [ ] Justify method choice vs alternatives

---

### 2.5 Results and Analysis (20% of Report)

**Criterion**: Were results complete and addressed research questions? Interpretations separated from observations? Ready for publication?

**What to include**:
- Clear presentation of all results
- Results organized around research questions
- Raw observations separate from interpretation
- Appropriate visualizations
- Statistical analysis where relevant

**Questions for yourself**:
- Do my results actually answer my research questions?
- Am I clearly separating "what happened" from "what it means"?
- Are my figures clear and informative?
- Have I reported negative/null results honestly?

**Structure suggestion**:
1. Overview of experiments run
2. Results for each research question/hypothesis
3. Observations (factual, what the data shows)
4. Analysis (interpretation, what it means)

**Actions**:
- [ ] Plan results structure before running experiments
- [ ] Create clear, labeled figures
- [ ] Report all results, not just favorable ones
- [ ] Clearly distinguish observation from interpretation

---

### 2.6 Language and Structure (10% of Report)

**Criterion**: High standard of English? No spelling/grammar errors? Sufficient structure and explanation?

**What to include**:
- Clear, professional academic English
- Logical flow between sections
- Appropriate section structure
- No errors

**Actions**:
- [ ] Use spell-check and grammar tools
- [ ] Read aloud to catch awkward phrasing
- [ ] Ensure logical flow between paragraphs
- [ ] Have someone else proofread

---

### 2.7 Clarity of Conclusions (10% of Report)

**Criterion**: Results discussed critically in view of research question? Sound conclusions drawn?

**What to include**:
- Direct answer to research questions
- Critical discussion of limitations
- Comparison to theoretical predictions
- Honest assessment of what was and wasn't achieved
- Future work directions

**Questions for yourself**:
- What is my answer to the research question?
- What are the limitations of my conclusions?
- How does this relate back to the Neorealism/Constructivism debate?
- What would strengthen/weaken my conclusions?

**Actions**:
- [ ] Directly address each research question in conclusion
- [ ] Discuss limitations honestly
- [ ] Avoid overclaiming
- [ ] Suggest concrete future work

---

### 2.8 Layout (5% of Report)

**Criterion**: Pictures, figures, graphs, tables produced to correct standard? Layout improves readability?

**What to include**:
- Professional figures with clear labels
- Consistent formatting
- Appropriate use of whitespace
- Numbered figures/tables with captions

**Actions**:
- [ ] Use consistent figure style
- [ ] Ensure all figures are referenced in text
- [ ] Check that figures are readable at print size
- [ ] Use LaTeX properly for professional layout

---

## 3. PRESENTATION (15%)

*Prepare this separately as you approach the defense.*

### 3.1 Context (15% of Presentation)
- Introduce subject in scientific context
- Clear motivation with real-world application/impact

### 3.2 Contents (25% of Presentation)
- Accurate description of work
- Clear research question
- Explicit statement of YOUR contribution

### 3.3 Structure and Narrative Style (25% of Presentation)
- Easy to follow
- Logical sequence
- Clear narrative arc

### 3.4 Discussion/Defence (25% of Presentation)
- Listen to questions carefully
- Answer concisely and to the point
- Demonstrate deep understanding

### 3.5 Delivery (10% of Presentation)
- Engaging manner
- Appropriate pacing
- Professional demeanor

---

## Checklist Summary

### Before Submission, Verify:

**Research Work** (demonstrated through process):
- [ ] I can explain all theory without notes
- [ ] I can explain all my code without help
- [ ] I have documented independent decisions
- [ ] I can defend my original contributions

**Report Content**:
- [ ] Clear motivation and scientific context
- [ ] Comprehensive, well-cited literature review
- [ ] Explicit, answerable research question(s)
- [ ] Detailed, reproducible methods with justification
- [ ] Complete results addressing all research questions
- [ ] Observations separated from interpretations
- [ ] Critical discussion and sound conclusions
- [ ] Honest limitations discussion

**Report Quality**:
- [ ] No spelling/grammar errors
- [ ] Logical structure and flow
- [ ] Professional figures and layout
- [ ] Complete, correctly formatted references

**Presentation** (prepare separately):
- [ ] Clear narrative arc
- [ ] Explicit contribution statement
- [ ] Prepared for likely questions
- [ ] Practiced delivery
